Lower Everest Region • 6 Days • Max: 4,065 m

Pikey Peak Trek: Clear Views Across Lower Everest

Sir Edmund Hillary once described the view from Pikey Peak as one of his favorites in Nepal. Situated in the lower Solukhumbu district south of Lukla, this six-day circuit does not require mountain flights. You reach the trailhead by jeep from Kathmandu to Dhap.

The trail passes through Buddhist monasteries at Junbesi, old pine forests, and high ridge crests. From the peak summit at 4,065 meters, on a clear morning you see a continuous horizon from Dhaulagiri in the west all the way past Everest, Lhotse, and Makalu to Kanchenjunga in the far east. Lodge accommodation is simple and uncrowded compared to the main highway to Base Camp.

Annapurna Foothills • 5 Days • Max: 3,313 m

Mohare Danda: Community Lodges Near Pokhara

For walkers looking for a quieter alternative to the busy stone stairs of Poon Hill, Mohare Danda offers an eco-community lodge trail along the ridge immediately west of Ghorepani. Initiated by local educator Mahabir Pun, income from the lodges directly finances village schools and wireless community network stations.

The trail starts from Galeshwor near Beni and climbs through terraced farms and oak forest up to the 3,313-meter ridge. You sleep in community dining halls with wood stoves, eating vegetables grown in community gardens. The sunrise panorama looks straight across the Kali Gandaki gorge at the south face of Annapurna I and Dhaulagiri.

Planning Advice • Nepal Regulations

How Many Trekking Routes Are in Nepal?

Nepal has dozens of marked trails, but from a planning standpoint the Department of Tourism divides them into three major categories:

  • Open Teahouse Routes: Langtang, Annapurna Sanctuary, and Khumbu. These routes have established lodges every two to three hours of walking. You need standard national park permits and TIMS cards.
  • Restricted High-Altitude Zones: Upper Mustang, Manaslu Circuit, Nar Phu, Dolpo, and Kanchenjunga. These areas require a special government permit, a minimum group size of two travelers, and an accredited licensed guide.
  • Wilderness Camping Trails: Remote paths across the Far West (Humla, Rara, Dhorpatan) where lodges do not exist and self-sufficient camping teams are required.

For first-time visitors to Nepal, the open teahouse trails provide the best balance of safety, cultural contact, and reasonable logistical costs.

December Travel • Gear & Safety

Trekking in Nepal in December: Cold Skies and Quiet Trails

December brings the start of cold weather across the Himalayas. While higher passes like Thorong La (5,416 m) or Cho La (5,420 m) may close due to sudden snowfall, lower and mid-elevation routes offer exceptional walking conditions.

Daytime skies in December are almost consistently clear, with sharp visibility and very little haze. However, temperatures drop sharply once the sun dips behind the ridges. Lodges above 3,000 meters do not have heated bedrooms; only the communal dining room has a central stove during dinner hours.

If you plan to trek in December, carry a down sleeping bag rated to at least -15°C, a sturdy insulated flask for hot water, thermal base layers, and microspikes for icy morning trail patches.
